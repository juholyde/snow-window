# Snow Window · second-pass illustration brief

Draft for art direction review. Generate each illustration from a fresh drawing. Use the first approved drawing as the visual reference for the set.

## Shared prompt

> Draw an original illustration for Snow Window, a ski-trip board for friends. Imagine a character animator commissioned to illustrate a Spanish Pyrenees travel poster. The moment should feel observed and funny. Build the silhouette around one clear gesture. Use long, confident curves, a few deliberate corners and open space between overlapping forms. Give the outer contour the most weight, thicken it where forms overlap, and taper interior marks to a fine point. Let two adjacent colors describe an edge wherever an ink line would do little work.
>
> Light the subject from the upper left with pale apricot winter sunlight. Group the forms into a light family and a cool shadow family. Use broad, clean areas of color and one designed shadow shape per major form. Give the strongest light–dark contrast to the face or focal point. Choose three to five main colors for this particular image. Repeat one accent in a small secondary detail if it helps the eye travel. Keep the surface clean, with no airbrush, simulated grain or bevel effects.

## Proposed color script

| Role | Suggested color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink | Midnight indigo `#24243D` | Silhouettes and the deepest overlaps |
| Snow in sunlight | Warm ivory `#FFF1D6` | Faces of snow and small highlights |
| Snow in shade | Periwinkle `#ABB8ED` | Turn snow away from the light |
| Mountain midtone | Alpine teal `#287C83` | Separate middle-distance ridges from distant violet |
| Distance | Heather violet `#746A9C` | Quiet atmospheric shapes |
| Warm light | Apricot `#FFBD68` | Sunlit fur, sun face or a strip of sky |
| Signature accent | Vermilion `#F06435` | Scarf, board or a shadowed sunray |

These swatches guide the artwork. Sample and inspect the generated colors before export. Preserve smooth edge alpha and intentional color relationships during optimization.

## Append one direction to the shared prompt

| Asset | Direction |
|---|---|
| Rufus | Catch the instant the board bites into a rightward carve. His back paws press the tail while his shoulders twist toward us. Both ears sit fully inside the snug teal beanie. Shape his wheaten beard with three or four tapered tufts. Leave sky-shaped gaps between his legs. One continuous vermilion board arc grounds the pose. Transparent cutout, including the whole board. |
| Tycoon skier | A portly original gentleman hurtles right, trying to maintain his dignity. His chin leads, knees compress, and the hat tips back one beat behind his body. Sweep the scarf and coat tails left in different arcs. Separate both wooden ski tips and keep the pole shafts clear of his face. White handlebar moustache, monocle and an indigo tailcoat. Distinctive angular profile, independent of any existing board-game mascot. Transparent cutout including all equipment. |
| Sun | Give the sun the expression of someone who has just spotted a friend: one brow lifts behind a smoked monocle, the opposite cheek pushes into a wink, and the smile tips to one side. Shape the brim with one curved ray and the tilted crown with three broad rays. Apricot face, vermilion rays, indigo lens and one ivory glint. Transparent cutout. The hat, lens and smile must read at 90 px. |
| Pyrenees panorama | Compose an opaque 3:1 view inspired by the Aneto–Maladeta massif. A broad upper snowfield breaks into granite ribs and drops into a glacial saddle. Put the main summit in the right third. Leave the upper left quiet for a heading. Separate three planes through color and value: muted violet distance, teal granite and warm ivory snow cut by periwinkle shadows. Reserve outlines for a few foreground rock breaks. Leave clear sky for the separate sun character. |
| Window symbol | Draw a slightly bowed cabin window with a sturdy sill and one curved mullion. Inside, a single slope cuts diagonally across a warm rising sun. Let one corner carry the weight of a small cap of snow. Express character through the tilt and proportions of the frame. Keep the sky, peak and sun distinct at 24 px. Transparent exterior. |
| Snow Window wordmark | Hand-letter exactly “Snow Window” on one line. Give the S a sweeping ski-turn curve and echo that angle in the two W forms. Draw open counters, optically even gaps and a clear word space. Let one snowcap settle onto a broad stroke. Use indigo lettering with ivory edging or highlights and one vermilion detail. Check the lettering at a 200 px header width. For the final lockup, place the approved window symbol beside the lettering as the same artwork. |

## First proof and delivery

Start with Rufus. Inspect the full drawing and a 128 px thumbnail on the site's dark sky and pale snow backgrounds. The eye should find his expression first, follow his lean into the carve, and still distinguish his paws from the board. Check for accidental tangencies and fuzzy cutout edges. Use the approved proof to match line character, light and color in the other assets.

Retain the established production filenames and dimensions. Export a 512 px wide mobile copy of each, keep every PNG below 600 KB and verify transparency for all cutouts. Record the actual generation prompts and export details in the artwork README.
